\#\#\#\# 4\. Cosmological Reach & The "Six-Hour" Energy Budget Calculating intergalactic trajectory limits requires a cosmological framework described by the **Friedmann Metric** with de Sitter-like dark energy acceleration. Comoving coordinates account for the spatial expansion of the universe over time. The fundamental cosmological parameters are defined as: \* Hubble Parameter: \$H\_0 \= 71 \\text{ km/s/Mpc}\$ ( \$2.3 \\times 10^{-18} \\text{ s}^{-1}\$ ) \* Dark Energy Density: \$\\Omega\_\\Lambda \= 0.73\$ \* Matter Density: \$\\Omega\_m \= 0.27\$ *Pedagogical Note:* Because dark energy dominates the current cosmic density budget ( \$\\Omega\_\\Lambda \= 0.73\$ ), space expands exponentially over time. This exponential acceleration establishes a **comoving cosmological event horizon** . Galaxies receding beyond this horizon travel faster than light relative to the launch point, placing a firm physical limit on the maximum distance probes can reach regardless of time. Solving the geodesic equations of the Friedmann metric yields the maximum reach boundaries and total accessible target galaxies for various launch velocities: \* **At** **\$50\\%c**\$ **Launch Speed:** Maximum reach \= \$1.24 \\text{ Gpc}\$ ( \$\\sim 1.16 \\times 10^8\$ reachable galaxies). \* **At** **\$80\\%c**\$ **Launch Speed:** Maximum reach \= \$2.33 \\text{ Gpc}\$ ( \$\\sim 7.62 \\times 10^8\$ reachable galaxies). \* **At** **\$99\\%c**\$ **Launch Speed:** Maximum reach \= \$4.09 \\text{ Gpc}\$ ( \$\\sim 4.13 \\times 10^9\$ reachable galaxies). **Key Insight: The "Six-Hour" Universal Energy Budget** Accelerating 30-gram payload probes (inclusive of full deceleration mass and particle redundancy) to **every single reachable galaxy in the observable universe** ( \$4.13 \\times 10^9\$ target galaxies at \$99\\%c\$ ) requires an absolute energy expenditure equivalent to **less than 6 hours (** **\$21,600 \\text{ seconds}**\$ **)** of the Sun's power output harvested by a Dyson swarm. Evaluated across specific propulsion architectures, the required solar energy collection times are: \* **No-Rocket Coasting Probes (** **\$99\\%c**\$ **):** \$1,430 \\text{ seconds}\$ ( \$\\sim 24 \\text{ minutes}\$ ) \* **Fission-Powered Probes (** **\$50\\%c**\$ **):** \$1,780 \\text{ seconds}\$ ( \$\\sim 30 \\text{ minutes}\$ ) \* **Antimatter-Powered Probes (** **\$99\\%c**\$ **):** \$8,310 \\text{ seconds}\$ ( \$\\sim 2.3 \\text{ hours}\$ ) \* **Fusion-Powered Probes (** **\$80\\%c**\$ **):** \$21,600 \\text{ seconds}\$ ( \$\\sim 6.0 \\text{ hours}\$ ) Even if we adopt the ultra-conservative 500-metric-ton replicator upper bound, the total Dyson swarm collection time required to power a universal launch across all reachable galaxies ranges between **754 and 11,400 years** —an interval that remains trivial on astronomical timescales. This extreme physical and energetic ease of universal expansion shifts our focus from technological feasibility to the game-theoretic motives of extraterrestrial species.
