\#\#\#\# 2\. Theoretical Tooling: Exploratory Engineering & Universal Replicators Assessing the technological bounds of distant civilizations without resorting to speculative science fiction requires the rigorous methodology of **Exploratory Engineering** . Formalized by Eric Drexler and others, exploratory engineering evaluates what is theoretical feasible under known laws of physics and thermodynamics, strictly separating physical impossibility from current human manufacturing limitations.  PHYSICAL LAWS (Known Bounds) | \+---------------------------+---------------------------+ | | v v Currently Feasible Exploratory Engineering (Human Technology) (Physically Permitted) \- Silicon Microprocessors \- Diamondoid Isotope Storage \- Chemical Propulsion \- Molecular Assemblers \- Manual Spaceflight \- Automated von Neumann Probes  Exploratory engineering rests upon two core postulates: 1\. **Natural Mechanism Replication:** Any physical or biological phenomenon occurring in nature (such as genetic self-replication, enzymatic chemistry, or human-level intelligence) can eventually be replicated under technological control (e.g., synthetic biology, molecular assemblers, artificial intelligence, or brain emulation). 2\. **Full Automation:** Any performable mechanical or computational task can be fully automated. Once a machine system is engineered to execute a physical process, an automated system can be built to manufacture and operate that machine without human oversight. Applied to space exploration, these postulates establish the physical reality of **von Neumann Probes** —automated, self-replicating universal constructors capable of traveling to a target destination, harvesting local raw materials, and manufacturing exact copies of themselves along with auxiliary industrial infrastructure. In analyzing probe architecture, we must maintain a strict conceptual boundary between the **replicator** (the lightweight universal constructor payload that executes manufacturing upon arrival) and the **probe** (the overall launch assembly, including reaction engines, shielding, and structural housing).
